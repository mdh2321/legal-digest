🇰🇷 South Korea

AI Basic Act Now in Force - Compliance Period Begins

OneTrust, Kim & Chang, Future of Privacy Forum

Now Effective AI Compliance

South Korea's AI Basic Act became effective on January 22, 2026, making it Asia-Pacific's first comprehensive AI law and the second globally after the EU AI Act. The law now requires operators of "high-impact" AI systems in critical sectors to implement mandatory notification, impact assessments, and risk management systems with human oversight. Key obligations include watermarking for AI-generated content and appointing a domestic representative for foreign companies operating AI in Korea. A one-year grace period focuses on guidance rather than penalties, but organizations should begin compliance efforts now. Fines up to KRW 30 million (~$20,870) apply for violations.

What This Means for You

  • Conduct a classification review to determine if AI systems qualify as "high-impact"
  • Establish a domestic representative in Korea if operating AI systems without a local presence
  • Implement content watermarking systems for generative AI outputs

🇸🇬 Singapore

PDPC Issues Multiple Enforcement Decisions Including $17,500 Fine

PDPC Singapore

Enforcement Data Privacy Security

Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) issued multiple enforcement decisions on January 8, 2026. A financial penalty of $17,500 was imposed on Singapore Data Hub Pte Ltd for failing to implement reasonable security arrangements to protect personal data in its possession. Additional decisions addressed breaches related to Protection and Accountability obligations. The PDPC continues its robust enforcement posture, with penalties of up to S$1 million or 10% of annual turnover available for serious data breaches. Organizations are reminded that Singapore's sector-led approach to AI governance means existing data protection rules apply fully to AI systems processing personal data.

What This Means for You

  • Review security arrangements for personal data under PDPC's Protection Obligation
  • Ensure Accountability Obligations are met with proper documentation and DPO appointment
  • Consider PDPC's AI Guidelines when deploying systems using personal data

🇦🇺 Australia

OAIC Privacy Compliance Sweep Underway Targeting 60 Entities

OAIC, Maddocks, National Law Review

Enforcement Data Privacy Active Now

Australia's Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) is conducting its first-ever compliance sweep in January 2026. The sweep targets approximately 60 entities across six sectors including pawnbrokers and second-hand dealers where in-person data collection creates information asymmetries. Non-compliant privacy policies may result in penalties up to AUD $66,000 through new infringement notice powers. This follows the landmark $5.8 million penalty against Australian Clinical Labs in October 2025 - Australia's first civil penalty under the Privacy Act. The sweep signals intensified enforcement focus heading into 2026, with the Children's Online Privacy Code consultation expected in Q1.

What This Means for You

  • Audit privacy policies now against Australian Privacy Principles, especially APP 1.4
  • Prepare for Children's Online Privacy Code requirements if handling children's data
  • Review automated decision-making disclosures ahead of December 2026 deadline

🇭🇰 Hong Kong

Code of Practice Issued Under New Critical Infrastructure Cybersecurity Law

Mayer Brown, Ashurst, Baker McKenzie

New Guidance Cybersecurity Critical Infrastructure

On January 1, 2026, the Office of the Commissioner of Critical Infrastructure (Computer-system Security) issued a Code of Practice under Hong Kong's new cybersecurity law. The Protection of Critical Infrastructures (Computer Systems) Ordinance, which took effect January 1, now has operational guidance for 8 critical sectors: energy, IT, banking, air/land/maritime transport, healthcare, and telecommunications. Two Designated Authorities have been named - the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and Office of the Communications Authority. Critical infrastructure designations will begin mid-2026 in a phased manner, with a six-month compliance period following designation. Penalties range from HKD 500,000 to HKD 5 million.

What This Means for You

  • Review the Code of Practice to benchmark cybersecurity governance and controls
  • Assess operations against the 8 designated critical infrastructure sectors
  • Prepare for designation process expected to commence mid-2026

🇯🇵 Japan

Active Cyber Defense Law Implementation Progressing

Baker McKenzie, Center for Cybersecurity Policy, Nippon.com

New Law Cybersecurity Nov 2026

Japan's Active Cyber Defense Law, passed in May 2025, is progressing toward full implementation in 2026. The landmark legislation grants the government authority to monitor foreign internet traffic traversing domestic infrastructure for threat detection and enables preemptive offensive cyber operations. A new Cyber Council is expected to launch soon to enhance public-private threat intelligence sharing. Critical Infrastructure Operators face new obligations: they must notify regulators when introducing certain "Critical Systems" and report cybersecurity incidents to both relevant ministries and the Prime Minister's Office. Incident reporting obligations take effect by November 2026.

What This Means for You

  • Assess whether operations qualify as Critical Infrastructure under the new framework
  • Prepare incident reporting procedures for November 2026 deadline
  • Review data flows through Japan infrastructure given new monitoring authorities

🇳🇿 New Zealand

Privacy Amendment Act: New Notification Requirements from May 2026

Bell Gully, Hunton, IAPP

New Requirement 1 May 2026 Data Privacy

New Zealand's Privacy Amendment Act 2025, signed into law in September 2025, introduces Information Privacy Principle 3A (IPP 3A) effective May 1, 2026. Organizations must now inform individuals when their personal information is collected indirectly - from sources other than the individual themselves. This closes a gap identified by the EU during New Zealand's adequacy assessment and brings notice obligations to global standards. Separately, the Biometrics Processing Privacy Code 2025 requires compliance by August 3, 2026 for organizations using facial recognition and other biometric technologies. The Customer and Product Data Act 2025 also enables greater cross-business data sharing.

What This Means for You

  • Map data collection sources to identify indirect collection requiring IPP 3A notices
  • Update privacy notices to cover indirect collection scenarios by May 1
  • Review biometric processing practices for Code compliance by August 3

🇹🇭 Thailand

PDPA Enforcement Intensifies with THB 21.5 Million in Fines

Tilleke & Gibbins, IAPP, Chambers

Enforcement Data Privacy PDPA

Thailand's PDPC announced on August 1, 2025 that it had issued eight new administrative fines totaling approximately THB 21.5 million (~$654,690) in five cases involving both public and private entities. This follows the landmark THB 7 million maximum fine imposed in August 2024 against a major online retailer for data leaks to call center scams. The enforcement actions reflect the PDPC's 2024-2027 Master Plan to strengthen PDPA enforceability. Organizations face fines up to THB 5 million (~$145,000) and potential criminal penalties including imprisonment. The PDPC's intensified posture signals that PDPA compliance is "no longer optional."

What This Means for You

  • Appoint a Data Protection Officer if handling significant volumes of personal data
  • Implement security measures to prevent data leaks to scam operations
  • Establish prompt breach notification procedures as required under PDPA

🌎 ASEAN

DEFA Negotiations Enter Final Phase - Q1 2026 Target

World Economic Forum, Allen & Gledhill, Nation Thailand

Upcoming Agreement Cross-Border AI

Thailand, as chair of DEFA negotiations, is pushing to finalize the Digital Economy Framework Agreement by Q1 2026, with formal signing expected in the Philippines later this year. The world's first region-wide digital economy agreement will harmonize rules for cross-border data flows, e-commerce, cybersecurity, and digital payments across ASEAN's 680 million-person market. Negotiations are over 70% complete with five priority areas: financial services, electronic transmissions, non-discriminatory digital products, undersea cables, and digital payment flexibility. DEFA could boost the region's digital economy from $300 billion to $2 trillion by 2030 and save businesses $15-20 billion annually in compliance costs.

What This Means for You

  • Monitor DEFA finalization for opportunities to streamline ASEAN data transfers
  • Engage with industry associations on DEFA implementation as details emerge
  • Plan for harmonized e-commerce and digital payment regulations

🇮🇳 India

DPDP Implementation Continues - Consent Manager Registration Opens November

IAPP, EY, National Law Review

Phased Implementation Nov 2026 Data Privacy

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act continues its phased rollout following the finalization of implementing rules in November 2025. The Data Protection Board was instituted in Stage 1 (November 13, 2025). Stage 2 begins November 13, 2026 with the consent manager registration process. Full implementation including consent, privacy notice, and security requirements arrives May 13, 2027. Key requirements include 72-hour breach notification, verifiable parental consent for children's data, and enhanced obligations for Significant Data Fiduciaries including annual DPIAs and algorithmic fairness assessments. Penalties reach up to INR 250 crore (~$30 million).

What This Means for You

  • Prepare consent management infrastructure for November 2026 registration
  • Implement 72-hour breach notification procedures
  • Assess whether operations may qualify as Significant Data Fiduciary
