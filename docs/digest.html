🇦🇺 Australia

Privacy Act Second Tranche Reforms in Development

Spruson & Ferguson, Ashurst, LexisNexis

Upcoming Reforms AI Data Privacy

Attorney-General Michelle Rowland has confirmed that a second tranche of Privacy Act reforms is being prepared for cabinet approval. The first tranche, passed in December 2024, introduced the framework for the Children's Online Privacy Code and a new statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy. The upcoming reforms will address the rise of generative AI, with Rowland noting current legislation is "not fit for the digital age." The OAIC has released its 2025/2026 regulatory action priorities following the Qantas cyber incident, and can now issue infringement notices up to $66,000 per contravention. Maximum civil penalties reach $50 million or 30% of adjusted turnover.

What This Means for You

  • Monitor second tranche reforms for new AI-related disclosure requirements
  • Prepare for Children's Online Privacy Code due December 10, 2026
  • Update privacy policies for automated decision-making disclosures by December 11, 2026

🇰🇷 South Korea

AI Basic Act Compliance - Foreign Companies Face Representative Requirement

Cooley, OneTrust, Future of Privacy Forum

Now Effective AI Extraterritorial

South Korea's AI Basic Act, effective since January 22, 2026, has extraterritorial application - it applies to AI systems outside Korea if they affect users or markets within the country. Foreign AI businesses without a Korean office meeting certain thresholds must designate a local representative. The law consolidated 19 separate AI-related regulatory proposals into one framework. Unlike the EU AI Act, Korea's approach features simplified risk categorization with no prohibited AI practices and comparatively lower financial penalties. Startups have expressed concerns that compliance requirements may hold back innovation, though Korea implemented its law faster than the EU, whose AI Act phases through 2027.

What This Means for You

  • Assess extraterritorial exposure if AI systems affect Korean users or markets
  • Appoint a domestic representative if required under the threshold criteria
  • Review AI classification against Korea's simplified risk framework

🇸🇬 Singapore

PDPC AI Guidelines and Three New Governance Initiatives

PDPC Singapore, IMDA, Norton Rose Fulbright

AI Data Privacy Guidance

Singapore continues its sector-led approach to AI governance rather than comprehensive AI legislation. The PDPC's Advisory Guidelines on AI Recommendation and Decision Systems remain key, encouraging use of anonymised data where possible. At the AI Action Summit in February 2025, Singapore announced three new initiatives including the Global AI Assurance Pilot launched by IMDA and AI Verify Foundation to codify best practices for generative AI testing. The PDPC also introduced a Proposed Guide on Synthetic Data Generation for AI applications. Recent enforcement decisions from January 2026 show the PDPC maintaining its robust enforcement posture with penalties reaching S$1 million or 10% of turnover.

What This Means for You

  • Apply PDPC's AI Advisory Guidelines when deploying AI systems with personal data
  • Consider synthetic data generation to reduce privacy risks in AI training
  • Use the Model AI Governance Framework and ISAGO for self-assessment

🇭🇰 Hong Kong

Critical Infrastructure Designations to Commence Mid-2026

Mayer Brown, Ashurst, China Briefing

New Law Cybersecurity Mid-2026

Hong Kong's Protection of Critical Infrastructures (Computer Systems) Ordinance, effective January 1, 2026, is now operational with the Code of Practice issued by the new regulator. The Office of the Commissioner of Critical Infrastructure (Computer-system Security) will begin designating critical infrastructure operators mid-2026 in a phased manner. Designated operators have a six-month compliance period following designation. The law covers 8 critical sectors with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and Office of Communications Authority as Designated Authorities. Penalties range from HKD 500,000 to HKD 5 million, with daily fines of HKD 50,000-100,000 for continuing breaches. The law is comparable to the EU NIS2 Directive.

What This Means for You

  • Assess likelihood of designation across the 8 critical infrastructure sectors
  • Use the Code of Practice to benchmark cybersecurity controls proactively
  • Prepare for six-month compliance window following any designation notice

🇯🇵 Japan

Active Cyber Defense - Cyber Council and Incident Reporting by November 2026

ICLG, Baker McKenzie, Center for Cybersecurity Policy

Implementation Cybersecurity Nov 2026

Japan's Active Cyber Defense Act, passed May 2025, structures Japan's cyber defense around four pillars: public-private collaboration, communications monitoring, counter-access to attack sources, and neutralization by authorities. The Cybersecurity Council will be re-established by November 2026 with involvement from the Prime Minister, essential infrastructure providers, and IT vendors. Incident reporting obligations for specified essential infrastructure providers take effect by the same deadline. Organizations must report cybersecurity incidents to both the Cyber Council and relevant ministries. Data breaches continue to fall under existing APPI rules, but the new framework expands the scope of reportable incidents.

What This Means for You

  • Assess whether operations qualify as essential infrastructure under ACDA
  • Prepare dual reporting procedures for Cyber Council and relevant ministries
  • Review scope of reportable incidents beyond data breaches

🇮🇩 Indonesia

PDP Agency Formation Targeted for 2026 - Implementing Regulations in Final Stage

Rajah & Tann, Chambers, ASEAN Briefing

Authority Formation Data Privacy 2026

Indonesia's Personal Data Protection Agency (Lembaga PDP) is targeted to be formed and operational by 2026. The draft Government Regulation on PDP Law implementation is in the "harmonization stage" before finalization. Until the PDP Agency is established, the Ministry of Communication and Digitals (MOCD) handles supervision. With the two-year transition period ended October 17, 2024, full compliance with Law No. 27 of 2022 is now required. Companies face fines up to 2% of annual revenue, bans on data processing, or criminal liability. The law requires Data Protection Impact Assessments and DPO appointment for processing specific personal data.

What This Means for You

  • Ensure full PDP Law compliance as the transition period has ended
  • Monitor for PDP Agency formation and new implementing regulations
  • Review cross-border data transfer mechanisms for adequacy requirements

🇮🇳 India

DPDP Rules - Consent Manager Registration Opens November 2026

IAPP, National Law Review, MeitY

Phased Rollout Nov 2026 Data Privacy

India's DPDP Rules, notified November 13, 2025, are being implemented in phases. Stage 1 (November 2025) instituted the Data Protection Board. Stage 2 begins November 13, 2026 with consent manager registration. Stage 3 (May 2027) covers all remaining provisions including consent, privacy notices, and security requirements. The law introduces consent managers as single points of contact for data principals to manage consent - a unique feature compared to GDPR. 72-hour breach notification is required. Penalties reach INR 250 crore (~$30 million), with INR 200 crore for failure to disclose violations timely. Children are defined as under 18, broader than GDPR's under 16.

What This Means for You

  • Prepare consent management infrastructure ahead of November 2026 registration
  • Implement 72-hour breach notification procedures
  • Review children's data handling - India defines children as under 18

🌎 ASEAN

DEFA Expected to Lower Business Costs - Signing Targeted Q4 2026

BusinessWorld, World Economic Forum, Nation Thailand

Upcoming Agreement Cross-Border Q4 2026

The ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) is on track for signing in Q4 2026 in the Philippines following completion of negotiations earlier in the year. Businesses can expect lower compliance costs and smoother cross-border digital trade within five years. Divergent digital regulations currently cost ASEAN businesses $15-20 billion annually in compliance overhead. DEFA aims to eliminate this by harmonizing standards for data flows, e-payments, and digital product classification. As of August 2025, negotiations had concluded 60% of paragraphs covering personal data protection, cybersecurity cooperation, AI, and emerging technologies. The digital economy could reach $2 trillion by 2030.

What This Means for You

  • Anticipate harmonized data flow rules to simplify ASEAN operations post-2026
  • Engage with industry associations on DEFA implementation guidance
  • Plan for unified e-payment and digital product standards

🇳🇿 New Zealand

IPP 3A Deadline Approaching - Indirect Collection Notices Required May 1

Bell Gully, IAPP, Privacy Commissioner

1 May 2026 New Requirement Data Privacy

New Zealand's Privacy Amendment Act 2025 introduces Information Privacy Principle 3A (IPP 3A) effective May 1, 2026 - now less than 3 months away. Organizations must inform individuals when personal information is collected indirectly from sources other than the individual themselves. This closes a gap identified by the EU during New Zealand's adequacy assessment. Separately, the Biometrics Processing Privacy Code 2025 requires compliance by August 3, 2026 for organizations using facial recognition and biometric technologies. The Customer and Product Data Act 2025 also enables greater cross-business data sharing, creating both opportunities and compliance considerations.

What This Means for You

  • Map all indirect data collection sources before May 1 deadline
  • Update privacy notices to cover third-party data collection scenarios
  • Review biometric processing practices for August Code compliance
